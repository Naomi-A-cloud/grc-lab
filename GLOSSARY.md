# Plain English glossary

Every word explained twice: once in normal language, once using the shop.

Imagine a shop. It holds cash and a folder of customer details.

---

## The three core words

**Risk**
Something that could go wrong.
*Shop: someone could steal the cash.*

**Control**
Something you put in place to stop it going wrong.
*Shop: a lock on the door.*

**Evidence**
Proof that the control is really working.
*Shop: your written notes from the night you went and tried the handle.*

---

## Words about how bad a risk is

**Likelihood**
How often you'd expect it to happen. Scored 1 to 5.
*Shop: a shop on a quiet street, 2. A shop on a rough street with no lock, 5.*

**Impact**
How bad it would be if it did happen. Scored 1 to 5.
*Shop: losing £20 of petty cash, 1. Losing every customer's bank details, 5.*

**Risk score**
Likelihood times impact. A number out of 25. Used to rank worries against each
other, so you fix the worst one first.

**Inherent risk**
How bad it would be **with no lock at all**. The starting point.
*Shop: the door has no lock. Score 20.*

**Residual risk**
How bad it still is **after** your lock is fitted and you've checked it works.
*Shop: good lock, tested. Score drops to 8.*

The key bit: you only get to lower the score if the lock has actually been
**checked**. Someone saying "yeah the door's locked" changes nothing.

**Risk appetite**
How much risk the company has agreed it will live with, decided in advance.
*Shop: "we'll accept losing up to £50. Anything above that, we act."*
Deciding this beforehand stops people arguing about it in a panic afterwards.

---

## Words about what you do next

**Treatment**
Your decision about what to do with a risk. Four options:

- **Reduce** — fit a lock. (Most common.)
- **Accept** — decide it's not worth the bother, and write down that you chose that.
- **Avoid** — stop doing the risky thing altogether. Stop keeping cash on site.
- **Transfer** — make it someone else's problem to pay for. Buy insurance.

**Preventive control**
Stops the bad thing happening in the first place.
*Shop: a lock. The thief never gets in.*

**Detective control**
Doesn't stop it, but tells you it happened.
*Shop: a CCTV camera. You find out, but the cash is already gone.*

You usually want both. A lock can be picked, and then you want the camera.

---

## Words about proving it

**Audit**
Someone checks your work and forms an opinion on whether it holds up.
*Shop: your insurance company sends someone to inspect your security.*

**Assurance**
Confidence that things really are as claimed, backed by someone having checked.

**Finding** (also called an **exception**)
Something the checker found that wasn't right.
*Shop: "the back door was left unlocked on three nights in June."*

**Scope**
What you are and aren't looking at.
*Shop: "we're checking the shop floor and the safe. Not the delivery van."*
Without scope, no question has an answer.

---

## Words about the rules

**Framework**
Someone else's ready-made list of sensible controls, so you don't start from
a blank page. Common ones: ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF.
*Shop: a shopkeepers' association checklist of "things every shop should have".*

**Policy**
A written rule the company has agreed to follow.
*Shop: "the safe must be locked whenever the shop is empty."*

**Control owner**
The named person responsible for a control actually working.
*Shop: "Sam locks up on weekdays."* A control with no owner is a wish.

---

## The one sentence to remember

Someone telling you the door is locked is not the same as you going and
trying the handle.
