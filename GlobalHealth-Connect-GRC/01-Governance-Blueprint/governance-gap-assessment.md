# GlobalHealth Connect Governance Gap Assessment

## Objective

The objective of this assessment is to identify weaknesses in the current information security governance structure at GlobalHealth Connect and recommend improvements.

## Governance Gaps

| Governance Gap | Evidence | Risk | Business Impact | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No formal information security governance structure | GHC currently manages security through an ad-hoc approach. | Security decisions may be inconsistent, delayed or missed. | Increased security exposure and unclear accountability may affect patient information and business operations. | Establish a formal information security governance structure with clear roles, responsibilities and escalation paths. |
| Unclear security accountability following acquisitions | GHC has grown rapidly and acquired two organisations. | Overlapping or unclear responsibilities may cause important security activities to be missed. | Weak accountability could increase security and compliance risks across the enlarged organisation. | Define clear security roles, reporting lines and decision-making authority across GHC and the acquired organisations. |
| Limited formal response to the data-leakage near-miss | GHC has experienced a data-leakage near-miss. | The same or similar incident could occur again if lessons are not formally reviewed and corrective actions are not tracked. | A repeated incident could expose patient information, damage trust and create regulatory and financial consequences. | Establish a formal incident review and corrective-action process. Track remediation actions to closure and escalate significant risks to management. |
| Security risks may not be consistently assessed during organisational changes | GHC has undergone rapid growth and two acquisitions. | New systems, employees, processes and data may introduce security risks that are not properly identified. | Unidentified risks could affect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of patient information and business services. | Include security risk assessments as part of acquisition and organisational change processes. |
| Security and compliance responsibilities are not clearly defined | The scenario identifies the need for a stronger and more sustainable governance approach. | Important compliance and security activities may not have clear owners. | Missed responsibilities could result in control gaps, delayed remediation and compliance issues. | Assign clear ownership for security, risk and compliance activities and document responsibilities using a RACI matrix. |
| Limited evidence of ongoing security oversight and monitoring | The Board has identified that the current ad-hoc security approach is no longer sustainable. | Management may not receive consistent information about security risks, control performance and unresolved issues. | The Board may have limited visibility of security risks and may not be able to make timely decisions. | Establish regular security reporting to management and the Board using defined security metrics, risk indicators and escalation thresholds. |
